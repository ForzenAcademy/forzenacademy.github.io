Volume One · Chapter Nine

No One Cheered

Ultimos

“Recover the Archmage.”

The order came from the western street.

The woman who gave it wore no ceremonial mantle. A silver command cord crossed her black coat from shoulder to hip, fastened at six points with flat glass seals. Rain had darkened her short hair and run soot down one side of her face. She took in the old gatehouse junction without stopping: the burning roofs, the ruptured main, the civilians disappearing through the chain pits, Archmage Solmir enclosed inside white light.

Then she saw the black-glass ring in Ultimos’s hand.

“Secure the evidence focus,” she said. “Witnesses alive. Catastrophic actor may be killed.”

The fresh formations advanced.

Earth workers came first. Four of them carried portable anchors on their shoulders, each a black iron spike as long as Orin was tall. They drove the points into the streets beyond the fallen barriers. Stone closed around the shafts. Lightning teams fastened silver cable between them while wind mages crossed the roofs in pairs.

Every unit brought its own ward.

Nothing depended upon a single lamp, focus, or buried line.

They had seen enough of Solmir’s defeat to learn from it.

Ultimos closed his gauntlet around the comparison ring. He lifted the torn edge of his charcoal coat and placed the glass beneath the surviving fold at his waist, between white armor and the dark layer beneath it.

The silver-cord commander raised one hand.

“Release Archmage Solmir. Place the focus on the road. Kneel with both hands behind your head.”

Ultimos looked toward the four streets.

The reinforcements had formed around the people still trying to escape. The western and northern routes were almost clear, but the chain pits remained crowded. Kisaya had gone below again to move Sela past the weakest braces. The older wind Warden stood beside the opening, lowering children one at a time while two laborers kept the line from crushing forward.

Orin was still aboveground.

He had told himself he was watching the lamps. Most of them were broken or dark. He had told himself Kisaya needed someone to warn her if the pit shifted. The truth was simpler and more humiliating.

He did not want to take his eyes off Ultimos.

Pale light opened over the chain pit.

It formed a low plane from the weathered gatehouse foundation to the cooperage wall, leaving the entrances clear beneath its edges. Its geometry closed and locked into the ancient stone. Ultimos lowered one finger, and the protection remained.

“Stay below it,” he told Orin.

Orin stared at the cracked pauldron and the black burn across his breastplate. “What are you going to do?”

Ultimos turned toward the western formation.

“Withdraw from this junction,” he said. “Take your wounded. The Archmage remains.”

The commander did not lower her hand. “You are surrounded by officers of Veyr operating under maximum-threat authority.”

“Then your authority has brought you to an unfavorable position.”

Inside the enclosure, Solmir stepped toward the western wall.

“Do not negotiate with it,” he said.

The relief commander looked at him. She did not ask whether he had called the armored figure a man, an artifact, or an ancient prisoner.

“First rescue line,” she ordered. “Advance.”

Black wards moved into the junction.

They overlapped without joining, six curved surfaces carried by six separate officers. Earth rose in fitted blocks ahead of their boots. Wind turned above them, clearing smoke and loose debris from the route toward Solmir. Lightning traveled only within the formation, passing instructions from ward to ward without touching the dead city network.

Behind them, a second team aimed hooked chains toward the white enclosure.

Ultimos extended his empty hand.

Fine light touched the leading ward.

The officer carrying it braced. Two others accepted the pressure without connecting their spells. The pale line bent, searched along the ward’s edge, and found no shared anchor it could sever to collapse the rest.

The formation continued.

Ultimos could have studied each protection. Orin had watched him do harder things with Solmir’s array. He could have separated six claims, opened six spell lattices, and left six people standing in the road with dead focuses in their hands.

The pale line in front of his palm became straight.

Radiance filled it.

The beam crossed the rescue formation.

For an instant, the six wards shone more brightly than anything else in the junction. Then every protected surface, portable focus, earth block, uniform, and body within the line lost the relationships that allowed it to remain.

The street appeared through them.

Empty rain struck broken paving where six people had been advancing.

The hooked chains behind them fell from hands that no longer existed.

Someone in the crowd screamed.

The sound continued after breath should have ended it.

Orin knew the person making it. Not her name, but her face: a woman from Furnace Lane who sold onions from a basket whenever the market patrol allowed her to stand beside the cooperage wall. She had been close enough to see six officers become an unobstructed stretch of street.

The Wardens behind the vanished formation stared too.

One medical officer ran forward carrying a red case. She reached the place where the front rank had been and stopped with the case still open in her hands. Bandages, splints, and two sealed bottles waited inside it. There was no one left to use them on.

Orin remembered being six years old in spring floodwater, too small to keep his feet. A Warden had carried him three streets with one arm while holding a wind barrier above them with the other. The coat had looked much like these coats. Black wool. High collar. Civic seal stitched at the shoulder.

For most of Orin’s life, the uniform had meant someone capable would arrive when ordinary people could not solve a danger themselves.

Now capability had brought six people into the path of a white line.

Ultimos watched the surviving formations reorganize.

He did not look at the empty space.

The silver-cord commander lowered her raised hand.

Her formations moved faster.

Wind teams launched from the roofs. Earth anchors brightened at all four streets. Lightning passed between the portable cables in a white-blue square, and black fire rose behind Ultimos in three narrowing walls.

He walked forward.

The first wall reached him.

One dismissive motion opened the magic sustaining it, but ordinary heat continued through the place where the spell had been. Fire wrapped his damaged coat and ran along the white armor. Ultimos did not stop. The cloth burned away from his right side and exposed the layered plates beneath it.

A wind mage released a dozen iron spikes from the northern roof.

They crossed the last ten paces without active magic.

Ultimos turned his shoulder into them. Pale reinforcement flashed beneath the armor. Nine spikes broke or glanced aside. One entered the split in his pauldron and snapped. The remaining two struck the road behind him.

The roof team began a second cast.

A rigid edge of Meridian light extended from Ultimos’s right hand.

It resembled a blade only because a blade was the simplest name Orin possessed for something long, narrow, and built to divide whatever occupied its path. It had no guard and no ornament. Fine geometry enclosed a white center that did not reflect in the rain.

Ultimos crossed the distance to the western anchor.

He moved faster than armor should allow.

Earth rose beneath his leading foot. He stepped on the moving stone, drove it back into the street, and passed the Warden shaping it before the man completed his ward. The luminous edge cut through the portable anchor.

Iron separated without sparks.

The protected spell inside it opened from top to bottom. Lightning escaped into the wet road. The Warden staggered away with both hands raised.

Ultimos passed him.

He did not strike someone who was no longer casting.

The second anchor team maintained its ward.

The white edge crossed the barrier, the black iron spike, and the two mages behind both.

None of them reached the ground.

The northern roof team released its second cast.

Wind compressed around an iron cable stretched between two chimneys. The cable snapped taut and swept down toward Ultimos’s neck, carrying no cutting spell after the first instant. He ducked beneath it. The movement brought one knee close to the paving, and an earth worker tried to close stone around his lowered arm.

Ultimos drove the luminous edge into the road.

A straight cut opened through the rising earth. His free gauntlet caught the Warden’s focus before the stone around it finished taking shape. He crushed the black casing, turned, and used the fading magic inside it to pull the broken cable from the air.

The cable struck the roof team’s legs.

One mage fell behind the ridge. The other caught a chimney, lost her wind lattice, and hung above the alley by both hands.

Ultimos looked up at her.

She released the remaining spell and concentrated on the bricks beneath her fingers.

He left her there.

A fire worker attacked from the neighboring roof. Ultimos crossed the junction before the burning arc reached the place where he had been. His reinforced step broke a paving stone. He struck the wall beneath the caster with the flat of his white weapon.

The spell lattice inside the brick opened. The roof ward failed. The fire worker dropped through the awning over an empty stall and did not rise immediately, but he remained whole.

Ultimos was not killing everything he reached.

Only everything that continued to resist when he reached it.

Orin backed toward the chain pit.

Kisaya’s head appeared below the grate. “How many?”

He could not answer.

“Orin.”

“I don’t know.”

She climbed high enough to see past him.

Ultimos had reached the northern street. Warden formations closed from either side, spectacular in their coordination. Ice spread across the road in branching white roots. Wind carried black fire over it without allowing the two spells to touch. Lightning descended through clear channels between them.

For one heartbeat, Kisaya smiled.

The Wardens had taken Sela. They had made Orin and Kisaya run through half the city. They had searched roofs where the children once slept and turned every familiar door into a danger for whoever might open it. Now the officers who had treated the Ashward as something they could close and search at will were discovering a man who did not care what their badges permitted.

Ultimos raised the white edge.

The smile left Kisaya’s face before he brought it down.

He did not cut through the spells.

He cut through the formation.

A pale arc crossed the northern street at chest height. Wards opened. The fire lost direction. The ice remained on the ground because it was still ice.

The mages inside the formation disappeared with their magic.

Black coats collapsed where the edge had passed below some shoulders and above others. A woman at the rear remained intact. Her focus had failed before the strike reached her. She fell beside an injured officer, dragged him by the collar, and tried to pull him away.

Ultimos advanced.

The woman looked up. Terror closed her face. She threw one arm over the wounded man and raised a shaking ward with the other.

The barrier was barely large enough to cover them.

Ultimos’s weapon passed through it.

The two figures vanished from the wet street.

Kisaya gripped the iron edge of the grate.

“He could have stopped them,” she said.

Orin watched rain darken the place where the wounded officer had lain.

“I know.”

The older wind Warden heard them.

She had stopped beside the entrance to the chain pit. Her focus remained clipped to her wrist, but no wind gathered around it. She looked at Ultimos, then at the people below the white plane.

“Keep moving,” she told Kisaya.

The silver-cord commander called to her from the western street.

“Warden. Return to formation.”

The older woman lowered another child into the pit.

“There are civilians in the collapse line.”

“The route is assigned to auxiliary crews.”

“Then they can send one.”

The commander’s expression hardened. “Return to formation.”

The older Warden unfastened the metal clasp at her wrist.

She placed the focus on the road.

Ultimos looked at her as he crossed the junction.

He continued past.

That should have made Orin feel better.

It did not.

The western earth teams changed tactics.

Instead of maintaining another barrier, they tore blocks from the old cooperage wall and held them above the street. Wind accelerated the stones toward Ultimos. Both spells ended before impact.

The blocks became ordinary falling masonry.

Ultimos opened a plane between himself and the first. Stone burst across it. The second struck the plane’s unsupported corner and spun through. It hit his thigh hard enough to stagger him.

The third was not aimed at him.

It struck the ancient foundation beneath the white protection over the chain pit.

The pale plane jumped.

Every person below it looked up.

Ultimos turned.

The west formation released another block toward the same place.

His blade disappeared.

A line of white light assembled from the fingers of his left hand to the western anchor position.

The earth workers saw it.

Two abandoned their casts and ran. One dropped flat behind the iron spike. Three held the anchor and reinforced its ward.

“We have orders to recover the Archmage!” one of them shouted.

Ultimos’s hand remained extended.

“So did the men who closed the temples.”

The beam took the anchor, the remaining earth workers, and the lower half of the wall behind them.

The second stone fell without wind carrying it. It struck the paving short of the chain pit and broke into pieces.

The cooperage wall began to lean.

Ultimos raised his right hand. A pale frame closed around the failing corner and held it.

The movement cost him more than it should have.

Light flickered where the frame joined the street. The reinforcement along his cracked shoulder failed for half a breath, allowing the broken plate to sag away from the dark armor beneath it.

The silver-cord commander saw.

“Sustain physical pressure,” she ordered. “Do not engage his active structures. Make him hold the district.”

More stone lifted from the streets.

Wind accelerated roof tiles, iron hooks, shattered timber, and the empty cart from the northern lane. The spells released each object before it crossed the junction.

Ultimos stood between the projectiles and the chain pit.

White planes opened, failed, and opened again. Timber broke against his forearms. The cart struck him squarely and came apart around the armor. One wheel continued into the crowd line until the older Warden caught it inside a turning cushion of air and drove it into the ground.

The impact split the wheel and drove its iron rim through the paving beside her boot.

Below the pale protection, one of the chain-pit braces cracked.

Kisaya shouted from inside the passage. People pressed back toward the ladder, but there was no room to move. Sela took hold of a child with her uninjured arm and pulled him beneath the surviving crossbeam while the old man they had lowered into the pit earlier wedged his shoulder against the failing timber.

Orin dropped beside the opening.

“The south brace!” Kisaya called. “It’s coming out of the wall.”

He could see the join below her: rotted wood sliding from a pocket in the old stone. The white plane above them protected against attacks from outside. It could not make a neglected support remember strength it had lost years ago.

Orin seized the abandoned chain from Sela’s restraints and passed it down. Kisaya wrapped it around the brace one-handed. The older Warden caught the upper length, threaded it through the grate, and used a controlled wind current to pull while Orin and two laborers hauled beside her.

The brace returned to its stone pocket.

“Hold it!” Kisaya shouted.

“With what?” Orin asked.

Sela pushed one of the discarded restraint bands through the links and jammed it across the grate.

The same iron that had held her on the execution platform took the load.

The pit steadied.

There was no time to appreciate the improvement.

The commander pointed at her.

“Arrest that officer.”

Nobody moved to obey.

There were too many other things moving.

The older Warden kept both hands on the chain until the last civilians passed the weakened brace. Her discarded focus lay in water several paces away. Any officer could see that she had chosen her work.

Ultimos could see it too.

He returned his attention to the formations still attacking him.

At the southern street, a lightning team raised a narrow signal mast above the roofs. Black glass opened at its tip and turned toward the distant Spire.

Orin saw the first pulse enter it.

One low red light.

Then another.

The pattern was not an attack. It was a report.

“They’re sending something north,” he said.

Ultimos heard him.

The red cross turned toward the mast.

The silver-cord commander stepped into his path. Three mobile wards closed around her without sharing a common edge.

“You will not leave this district,” she said.

Ultimos released the plane in front of his body.

An iron hook struck his breastplate. The armor bent inward with a sound like a hammer against a crypt door. He caught the chain behind it before the links lost their remaining magic and pulled.

The Warden at the other end had anchored herself against movement.

Ultimos did not try to move her.

He moved the active chain.

It tore sideways through the first mobile ward, wrapped the portable focus maintaining the second, and dragged both structures into the third. Their claims collided. Black light burst between them.

Ultimos entered the opening.

The commander cast fire from one hand and ice from the other. The two elements curved around him toward his damaged shoulder.

His gauntlet struck the fire path. Pale light opened its direction and sent the spell upward. He allowed the ice to reach his forearm, seized its active lattice before she released it, and pulled.

The commander crossed the distance unwillingly.

Ultimos caught her by the throat.

Every ward around her flared.

He placed two fingers against the lowest glass seal on her silver cord.

“Your formations have failed.”

The commander’s boots scraped against broken stone. “They are still moving people out of your way.”

Ultimos glanced toward the western lanes.

Wounded Wardens were being carried north. Civilians moved behind them. Some officers remained in firing positions. Others held roofs, stopped water, or guided people toward open streets.

The commander forced the words through his grip.

“Release the Archmage. This ends.”

“You entered after watching him fasten a civilian into an execution spell.”

“I entered because the governor of Veyr is your prisoner.”

“And that absolves the formation?”

“It explains it.”

For the first time since the attack began, Ultimos paused.

Orin wanted the pause to matter.

The commander struck the black-glass seal beneath his fingers.

Lightning traveled through her silver cord and entered Ultimos’s gauntlet.

His hand closed.

The seal broke.

The entire command cord came apart. Glass fasteners separated from cloth. Lightning opened inside the commander’s own ward and threw her across the street.

She struck the base of a portable anchor and did not rise.

Ultimos continued toward the signal mast.

He had left her body behind.

Orin could not tell whether she was alive.

Three Wardens blocked the southern street.

Their focuses shook in their hands. One was bleeding from the forehead. Another had lost half his coat sleeve. The third looked toward the commander's unmoving body and lowered his ward.

“Drop them,” he told the others.

The woman beside him stared. “Solmir is still inside.”

“And we are not getting him out.”

She looked at Ultimos.

Her focus struck the road.

The last Warden held his lightning for one more breath, then allowed it to drain into the wet paving. He set the black rod down beside the others.

Ultimos stopped in front of them.

No one moved.

“Take the wounded,” he said. “Leave.”

They obeyed.

The mercy frightened Orin in a different way.

It proved the rest had not been a frenzy.

Ultimos had known the difference every time.

He simply considered a maintained spell sufficient reason to kill whoever held it.

The signal mast pulsed again.

A third red light climbed its length.

Ultimos extended one hand.

Light gathered.

The mast stood on the roof of a narrow workshop with rooms built above its rear half. The workers had evacuated the lower floor, but faces remained behind one upper window: a woman, two boys, and an old man trying to force open a swollen shutter.

Orin saw them.

“People are still inside!”

Ultimos changed the angle of his hand.

The beam crossed above the window.

It removed the mast, the rooftop Warden team, and the fortified parapet supporting them. Black glass vanished before the fourth pulse could form.

One red spark escaped north through the rain.

The roof remained for less than a second.

Then the upper corner of the workshop began to fold.

Ultimos raised both hands.

White supports appeared beneath the exposed beams. One locked into the wall. A second missed its intended join by the width of a finger and slipped across wet brick. The third formed with a broken angle and collapsed into sparks.

Binding strain had reached his precision.

The woman at the window pushed the two boys toward the stairs.

The old man freed the shutter.

The roof dropped.

One white support caught it. The load moved through the remaining wall instead. Brick burst outward into the street.

Orin ran.

The pale plane over the chain pit ended at his shoulder. He passed beneath its edge and crossed the flooded road while masonry struck around him.

Kisaya shouted his name.

The first boy reached the workshop door. The second followed with blood on one side of his face. Their mother stumbled out behind them.

The old man did not.

Orin climbed onto the broken lower wall. Dust filled the room. A timber lay across the stairs, pinned beneath half the roof. He could see one gray sleeve beyond it.

“Help me!”

The older wind Warden reached him first. She placed both hands beneath the timber and forced air into the narrow space below it. Orin pulled at the sleeve.

It moved without resistance.

The old man’s hand came into view.

Brick had crushed the rest of him beneath the landing.

No light crossed the street to restore him.

There was nothing living left for it to follow.

The older Warden lowered the timber.

The woman who had escaped the workshop came back through the dust before anyone could stop her. She saw the gray sleeve and made a sound too quiet to resemble the scream from earlier. One of her sons caught her around the waist when she tried to climb over the wall.

“He was behind us,” she said. “He was right behind us.”

Orin still had the dead man’s fingers in his hand.

They were warm.

He let them go.

Orin backed out of the workshop.

Ultimos was already facing the formations at the other three streets.

He had seen the collapse. Orin knew he had because the white supports remained beneath it, holding the roof high enough for the surviving family to escape.

He did not go to the body.

The Spire forces reorganized while he tried to preserve the workshop.

Portable barriers sealed the eastern and northern streets. A lightning emplacement rose above the chain pit on a scaffold of black iron. At the northwestern roofline, surviving officers built a command station around the final intact relay.

They had turned the junction into three fortified positions.

Ultimos looked at each one.

The luminous weapon in his hand had disappeared. Pale reinforcement along his armor flickered irregularly. His left pauldron hung open. The burn on his breastplate had widened into a black fracture.

None of it altered his posture.

Inside the enclosure, Solmir stood close to the northern wall.

“Now they have seen what you are,” he said.

Ultimos faced him across the ruined platform.

“They have begun to see what you preserved.”

The northern command station fired.

Ultimos did not raise a barrier.

Three circles opened behind him.

Each contained one straight construction of white light.

No intricate frame connected them. No articulated body directed them. Each lance had a path, a boundary, and one dismantling instruction.

The eastern bastion released fire.

The first lance crossed the street and removed the portable barrier, the anchors maintaining it, and the armed formation behind them. Flame escaped upward from a spell whose casters no longer existed.

The lightning scaffold discharged toward the chain pit.

The second lance reached it first.

Black iron, silver conductors, glass focuses, and the officers standing among them disappeared inside a vertical field of white. Lightning struck nothing and divided into the flooded street.

The final command station sent its report north.

Red light entered the relay.

The third lance crossed the roofs.

The black-glass structure vanished. So did the fortified position around it and the upper story upon which it had been built.

One fragment of red light had already left.

It traveled above the roofs toward the distant Spire.

The building beneath the command station shuddered.

Ultimos closed his hand. A pale frame appeared around the remaining walls, but the building had been constructed in stages he had never observed. Its old front resisted the newer rear. The left wall settled. The right tore away from the adjoining home.

People screamed inside the lower rooms.

Kisaya emerged from the chain pit and ran toward them.

Orin followed.

The older wind Warden was already there.

They pulled three people through a rear window while pale geometry held the worst of the weight. A fourth crawled out beneath a table with one leg bent wrong. Somewhere inside, a woman called a name until smoke closed her voice.

A surviving Warden entered through the front with a wet cloth over his mouth. He had dropped his focus, but the silver cord of the Spire remained across his coat. He returned carrying a girl whose hair had burned away on one side. The older wind Warden took the child from him without asking which formation he had belonged to.

Another officer found the woman who had been calling. They brought her out alive. The name she had shouted belonged to someone they did not find.

Orin and Kisaya pulled at stone until their nails split. Every few breaths the building shifted and pale lines flared overhead, holding weight that had nowhere safe to settle. The white geometry saved the rooms it understood. It could not tell them who had been inside before the walls came down.

Ultimos separated burning timber from the adjoining roof. The motion kept the fire from spreading and opened the damaged room to rain.

It did not return anyone trapped beneath the collapse.

At the center of the junction, the circle anchoring Solmir’s enclosure cracked.

One pale wall flickered.

Solmir looked down.

He moved one boot over the fracture before the light steadied again.

Orin saw the motion and understood only that the Archmage had found something worth hiding.

The fighting ended unevenly.

One street fell silent after its formation vanished. Another emptied as surviving Wardens carried their wounded north. At the western approach, officers lowered their focuses and backed away beneath a length of pale surrender cloth held between two poles.

Ultimos let them go.

Nobody attempted to recover Solmir again.

Rain fell through smoke and hissed against black fire left burning in broken timber. Water from the public main covered the road above Orin’s ankles. Pieces of the Liberation monument lay scattered beside the platform, its carved archmage split across the chest and the giant white hand broken a second time.

The medical officer with the unused red case had found work now. She knelt beside the person with the ruined leg and cut the trouser seam while a laborer held a board for a splint. Across the street, two Wardens covered three bodies with coats. For the vanished formations, there was nothing to cover.

No one tried to count the dead aloud.

The old gatehouse stones still stood.

Ultimos had preserved those.

He returned to the middle of the junction.

The pale lances were gone. Only the plane over the chain pit, the frames beneath damaged buildings, and Solmir’s enclosure remained. Each trembled at its edges. Light no longer met cleanly at the corners.

Ultimos’s damaged coat hung from one shoulder. His left pauldron remained open. Rain ran through the crack in his breastplate and down the layered white armor below it.

The helmet had not opened.

The red cross faced the Ashward.

Orin stood beside Kisaya near the collapsed building. Ash covered her cheeks. Blood from her reopened knuckles mixed with rain on her hand.

Before the battle, she had moved toward Ultimos whenever Wardens appeared.

Now she remained where she was.

Sela had climbed partway from the pit. The older Warden supported her beneath one arm. Around them, laborers, shopkeepers, children, injured officers, and families Ultimos had protected gathered among the wreckage.

No one approached him.

Solmir held the cracked side of his enclosure and watched.

“You buried my people beneath this city,” Ultimos said.

His voice did not need the lamps.

“You made their foundations your property. You made their works proof of your victory. You taught their descendants to thank you for the grave.”

The surviving Wardens at the ends of the streets did not move.

“I will open every seal you closed. I will recover every person who remains, every record you concealed, every practitioner you drove into darkness, and every fragment of the Order the Severance failed to erase.”

Orin heard the promise beneath the threat.

He believed Ultimos meant every word.

That was what made the rest unbearable.

“When there is nothing left for you to threaten, burn, or bury, I will answer what was done to us.”

The featureless helmet turned toward Solmir.

“The institutions built upon our stolen names will answer for every one.”

Four centuries of silence followed him into the rain.

No one cheered.
