Volume One · Chapter Seven

Come Out, White Mage

Ultimos

“Come out, white mage.”

The command entered the abandoned kiln through every broken place at once.

It spoke from the black seal inside the street lamp. It trembled through the chain stretched across the glassworks lane. Even the cracked firing trays seemed to hold the Archmage’s voice for a moment after the words ended.

Ultimos did not answer.

He stood beneath the fallen section of roof and watched the black emblem in the lamp. Its fire possessed no heat. Four narrow channels entered the glass housing from different directions, each carrying a separate elemental color until the seal consumed them.

“He’s using the containment network,” Orin whispered.

“Yes.”

Ultimos raised one hand toward the distant lamp without touching it. A pulse crossed the black emblem, traveled into the street, and divided beneath the paving.

“The voice is being repeated by the same structure that closes your roads.”

Solmir spoke again.

“The unidentified caster responsible for the attack upon the Hall of Liberation is ordered to submit to emergency examination. You have employed prohibited non-elemental effects, resisted civic security, and continued to travel with two wanted witnesses.”

Orin looked at Kisaya.

The Archmage had called him a white mage only when addressing the fugitive. For everyone else, Ultimos remained an unidentified caster using prohibited effects.

Either Solmir did not believe his own bait, or he did not want Veyr to hear him believe it.

“That’s careful,” Orin said.

Kisaya peered through the gap in the kiln wall. “He can be careful while threatening us. Impressive.”

“He has not threatened you yet,” Ultimos said.

Boots struck the lane outside.

The checkpoint Wardens lowered their chain and turned toward the main Ashward road. People were being pushed away from the narrower streets, not toward their homes but north, where the roofs opened around the old gatehouse junction.

Then Orin heard a woman shout.

It came from beyond the cooperage roofs. The words were lost beneath the scrape of shutters and the repeated order to clear the road, but he knew the voice.

He had heard it break into tears over a child’s healed hand.

Orin reached the wall before he remembered concealment. Kisaya caught the back of his coat and dragged him beneath the gap.

“That was her,” he said.

“Who?”

“The boy’s mother.”

The same recognition reached Kisaya a breath later. Her face emptied.

Ultimos had already crossed the kiln. “Where does that road lead?”

“The old gatehouse,” Kisaya said. “The monument is there.”

The weathered archmage breaking the giant white hand.

People had streamed around it without looking the night before.

Now the Conclave was driving half the Ashward toward it.

Ultimos studied the rooftops through the collapsed ceiling. “Show me a position from which the junction can be observed without crossing the street.”

Kisaya pointed toward the rear kiln wall. “There’s a joined roof above the shuttered cooperage. Ressa’s crew patched it last spring.”

“Can it bear me?”

She looked at the white armor concealed beneath his coat. “For a little while.”

Ultimos stepped onto the broken firing shelf. Kisaya went first, climbing through a split in the brick and finding holds Orin could not see. He followed her into a narrow yard where old clay pipes lay stacked beneath weeds. Ultimos came last. The damaged coat brushed the wall, reshaped cloth sliding over broken mortar without catching once.

They crossed a lean-to, an abandoned drying frame, and two roofs that complained beneath Ultimos’s weight. Wardens moved through the lanes below in groups of four. Each group carried a black-glass focus, and each focus answered the lamps with a low red pulse.

No one looked up. The danger had been ordered into the streets, so the Wardens searched doors.

Kisaya stopped behind a chimney at the cooperage roof.

The old gatehouse junction lay below.

It was not a square. Four working streets had simply widened around the remains of a stone arch and never narrowed again. The waist-high Liberation monument stood beneath it, rain-softened archmage and broken white fingers nearly lost beneath four centuries of soot.

A wooden proclamation platform had been raised beside the monument.

Sela knelt at its center.

Orin learned her name when Solmir spoke it to the district.

“Sela of Furnace Lane stands under emergency judgment. Yesterday afternoon, West Clinic recorded a severe cooking burn to her son’s hand. A medical inspection ordered after the Hall attack found that hand fully restored by prohibited practice. Sela has concealed the source, obstructed a maximum-containment inquiry, and refused to identify associates of the wanted witnesses.”

Her wrists were held apart by bands of black metal fixed to two upright posts. More bands crossed her ankles and waist. Red light traveled through them at the pace of a slow heartbeat.

Her son was nowhere in the junction.

Thank every merciful thing for that.

Wardens held the four streets in disciplined lines. Their wards overlapped without touching, leaving no clear approach. Earth mages had raised low barriers to divide the crowd from the platform. Wind workers stood on the surrounding roofs with black feathers tied beneath their collars. At every corner, a lamp had been opened and joined to the platform by temporary silver channels laid across the paving.

The entire junction had become a spell.

At its western edge stood Archmage Solmir.

Orin had seen his face stamped on proclamation sheets and rendered in black glass above public trials. The real man was older than those images admitted. Iron-gray hair lay close to his head, and the severe black coat beneath his mantle had been fastened to the throat despite the evening heat. His staff was taller than he was, dark wood enclosed at intervals by rings of black glass.

Nothing about him needed to announce power. Everyone else had arranged themselves around it.

Solmir faced the crowd rather than Sela.

“The sentence is suspended until the next district bell. It will remain suspended if the rogue caster presents himself inside the marked circle and submits without resistance.”

Black fire drew a ring in the street twenty paces from the platform.

The surrender point.

Orin saw silver beneath the fire. Earth lines crossed under it. Wind moved down instead of up. Whatever stepped into that circle would be attacked from every direction at once.

Sela lifted her head.

Her hair had come loose from its knot. A bruise darkened one side of her mouth, but her voice carried when a wind mage extended two fingers toward her.

“Don’t come.”

The crowd shifted.

Solmir looked back at her.

Sela pulled against the restraints. The red light quickened, and pain closed her voice for a moment. She forced the words through it anyway.

“Whoever you are—don’t. He’ll kill you too.”

The wind mage dropped his fingers. Sela’s voice vanished from the junction.

Solmir did not rebuke her. He turned toward the black ring and waited as though her refusal had been another expected part of the demonstration.

Orin’s hands had closed around the chimney brick.

The boy had opened and closed his restored fingers against Sela’s cheek. She had bowed to Ultimos before leaving Nema’s room. That was everything she had done.

Nema had wrapped the healed hand and warned them to let the clinic believe its treatment had failed.

It had not been enough.

The clinic had written the original injury beside Sela’s name and address. After the Hall, Solmir had ordered recent serious injuries in the Ashward inspected. Someone had made the boy remove his bandage and found a hand carrying neither scar nor stiffness.

No informer had needed to know Nema’s door. The official trail reached Sela and ended because she had chosen those restraints over giving them the next name.

Ultimos watched the red pulse pass through the bands around her.

“We leave,” he said.

Kisaya turned on him. “No.”

The word came loudly enough that Orin expected every Warden below to look up.

None did. Solmir’s voice had begun again, repeating the terms through the city.

Ultimos moved back from the chimney. “If I answer this demand, they will take another person tomorrow.”

“She is not a demand.” Kisaya followed him across the roof. “She is a person.”

“She is both. Solmir made that true when he placed her there.”

“So he gets to kill her?”

“He may kill her whether I submit or not. If I place myself within that circle, I surrender the archive, the other seals, every surviving practitioner I have not found, and the only opportunity to prevent the destruction of their records.”

His voice remained low. It was worse than anger. He had measured Sela against everything beneath the Spire and found the difference too large to discuss.

“If the threat succeeds once,” he continued, “every healer, witness, laborer, and child observed near us becomes a restraint they can fasten around my purpose. I will not teach him that it works.”

“You already taught him something,” Orin said.

Ultimos faced him.

Fear nearly returned the rest of the sentence to Orin’s throat.

Then Sela’s restraints pulsed below.

“You healed her son,” Orin said. “You chose to do it. They took her because the hand was perfect and she wouldn’t tell them where it happened.”

“Solmir chose to take her.”

“You said the danger created when you chose to use us stayed under your command.”

The red cross gave Orin nothing.

He pressed on.

“We brought you to Nema. You healed that boy in front of his mother. You restored the mural because you wanted Fermina’s face to be true. Solmir chose this, but he could only choose it because of what we did.”

“Responsibility does not require obedience to the person exploiting it.”

“We aren’t asking you to obey him.”

Orin’s voice had stopped shaking. He was too angry to manage fear and anger at the same time.

“You can survive the cost of refusing him. She can’t.”

Ultimos’s gauntlet closed once beneath the repaired sleeve.

Kisaya stepped between them. “We are not asking you to surrender. We’re asking whether your strategy is clever enough to save her.”

For a few seconds, the only sound on the roof came from the black seals repeating Solmir’s terms.

Ultimos looked from Kisaya to the junction.

“Anger is not a plan,” he said.

“Then ask us for one.”

His attention returned to the platform.

The silence changed. Orin had begun to recognize the difference between Ultimos refusing an argument and finishing with it.

“Where can the junction be entered without using those streets?” he asked.

Kisaya answered immediately. “Below.”

She dropped to one knee and traced the streets against the roof tile with her finger.

“The old gatehouse had chain pits under the arch. Most were filled when they put in the monument, but the east pit connects to a warming conduit behind the bakery. Ressa sent me through it when we repaired their oven flue. It comes up under the platform side of the junction.”

“Can you reach the woman from it?”

“If they haven’t sealed the grate.”

“Assume they have.”

“Then I open it.”

Ultimos looked at Orin. “The visible structure.”

Orin returned to the chimney and forced himself to study the restraints instead of the person inside them.

Four elemental marks repeated across the metal bands. Earth at the posts. Wind along the silver channels. A narrow fire path around Sela’s waist. The fourth mark appeared on every public emergency diagram taught in ward schools: a lightning fork used to carry one instruction through several linked stations.

“It reports through the lamps,” Orin said. “Like the barriers underground. Earth holds it, wind carries the signal, lightning makes every band answer together.”

“And the fire?”

Orin watched another pulse complete its circuit. The fire line darkened whenever the signal reached Sela.

“I don’t know.”

“Good.”

Orin stared at him.

“You identified what you understand and stopped before invention,” Ultimos said. “The fire path is incomplete.”

He extended two fingers toward the junction. No light appeared.

“It enters the bands but has no visible return. Solmir has concealed the execution channel within the containment signal.”

Kisaya looked over the chimney. “Can you break it?”

“I can break everything in that junction.”

“Quietly.”

“Not without understanding where the fire returns. If I separate the wrong protection, the system may complete its instruction through her.”

The first district bell sounded.

It was not the deadline. A single warning stroke rolled over the roofs, telling the Ashward that the final interval had begun.

On the platform, Sela closed her eyes.

Ultimos studied the nearest lamp, then the roof routes around the junction.

“How long?” he asked.

“Five minutes between warning and district bell,” Orin said. “Maybe less if the western tower is running fast.”

“Where does the nearest branch of the lamp network pass beneath us?”

Orin did not know.

Kisaya did.

She pointed to a copper gutter descending the cooperage wall. “That isn’t rainwater. It carries the message line to the tannery lamps. The real gutter is on the other side.”

Ultimos walked to it and placed one gauntlet against the darkened copper.

“The woman is exposed because I restored what should not have been left damaged,” he said. “Solmir will not determine the cost of that act.”

It was not an apology. He sounded as though he had identified an error in a battlefield account and reassigned the forces required to correct it.

Kisaya rose.

Ultimos pointed toward the eastern roofs. “Open the pit. Do not touch the restraints, the platform, or any metal joined to the lamps. When the bonds move, take her south through the conduit.”

“When they move how?”

“Down.”

Kisaya glanced at the street below. “That answer could use more answer.”

“It will be unmistakable.”

The sharp reply she might ordinarily have given never came. She checked the brace around her injured arm and crossed the roof at a crouch.

Ultimos turned to Orin.

“You will watch the lightning path. Tell me when its cadence changes.”

“What are you doing?”

“Waiting for Solmir to show me the portion he concealed.”

Below them, the black ring burned around an empty surrender point.

The second warning stroke began.

It rolled from the western tower before the first vibration had finished leaving the roof.

Kisaya crossed the next gap on a plank no wider than her foot. Her injured arm stayed pressed to her ribs. At the far side, she pulled the plank after her and vanished behind a row of chimney pots.

Orin counted the red pulses below.

One entered the western lamp.

The lightning marks on Sela’s restraints brightened together. Earth answered at the posts. Wind carried the acknowledgment back toward Solmir’s staff.

Six heartbeats later, it happened again.

“Six,” Orin said.

“Continue.” Ultimos kept his hand against the copper message line.

No white light marked what he was doing. His fingers remained motionless while the city’s magic trembled beneath them.

Orin counted another six.

Below, Sela shifted one knee on the boards. The fire line around her waist tightened. Her shoulders jerked, but she did not cry out.

“It hurts her every time.”

“The pulse tests whether the circuit remains closed.”

“Can you stop that part?”

“Not without beginning.”

Beginning would tell Solmir where they were.

Orin counted again.

On the roof north of the junction, two wind Wardens descended from a disk of turning air. Their coats snapped once and settled. One held a sketch copied from the Hall witnesses: a dark hood, a suggestion of broad shoulders, and a red cross drawn too large across the face beneath it.

“Mural enthusiast,” the younger Warden said. “Has to be.”

His companion kept her eyes on the roofs. “A mural enthusiast did not remove Captain Vara from her uniform.”

“Artifact, then.”

“The Hall report says no focus.”

“The report also says the wall repaired itself. Maybe the armor is the focus. Maybe there’s no one inside it.”

The older Warden glanced toward Solmir. “Whatever it is, the Archmage wants it breathing.”

Neither of them said they believed an ancient White Lord had walked out of the ruins.

The younger one pointed toward the cooperage roof.

Orin lowered himself behind the chimney.

Ultimos remained beside the false gutter.

“They’re coming here,” Orin whispered.

“Yes.”

“Are we moving?”

“No.”

Air touched the far edge of the roof.

Loose grit rolled uphill toward the Wardens. The older woman was searching for disturbed dust. Her spell traveled around the chimney pots and beneath the tiles, lifting everything light enough to remember a recent footstep.

It reached the route Kisaya had taken.

Ultimos removed his hand from the copper and placed two fingers against the roof.

The tile beneath them did not glow. A crack closed through the old clay. Dust settled into it. The neighboring tiles shifted by fractions, returning to the arrangement they had held before three fugitives crossed them.

The searching air reached the chimney.

Orin flattened himself against warm brick. A grain of grit lifted beside his cheek and spun once. His lungs had begun to ache when the wind withdrew.

“Nothing,” the younger Warden said.

“Check the next roof.”

Their boots crossed the ridge less than ten paces away.

Ultimos did not look after them. He returned his hand to the copper line.

The restraint pulse completed another circuit.

“Six,” Orin breathed.

Below, Solmir spoke to a black-glass focus beside the platform. His voice left the junction and entered every lamp in Veyr.

“One interval remains.”

The crowd had grown quiet enough that Orin could hear the metal restraints whenever Sela moved.

At the eastern edge of the junction, a bakery chimney released a sudden cloud of black soot.

No one below looked at it. Ashward chimneys did worse every evening.

Orin knew Kisaya had reached the warming conduit.

Three muffled knocks rose through the platform. A fourth followed after a pause, quieter than the others.

Kisaya had found the old fasteners and loosened them from below.

The third warning stroke sounded.

Ultimos’s fingertips pressed deeper into the copper.

“The next signal may differ before the bell,” he said. “Do not watch the fire. Watch the lightning.”

Orin fixed his eyes on the narrow forks engraved into the restraints.

The ordinary pulse arrived.

Every fork brightened at once.

One.

Two.

Three.

At the fourth, the light in the band around Sela’s left wrist lagged by half a breath.

“There.”

Ultimos did not move. “That was a test of the execution branch. It remains concealed.”

Orin swallowed. “Then what am I waiting for?”

“For Solmir to commit murder.”

The answer held no drama. Ultimos was telling him the condition under which the hidden spell would become legible.

On the platform, Sela opened her eyes.

She looked toward the surrounding roofs.

For one impossible moment, Orin thought she had found him. Her gaze passed over the cooperage chimney and continued to the bakery, where the old monument blocked her view of the conduit beneath her feet.

Sela lowered her head again.

Solmir approached the platform.

“You have protected someone who abandoned you,” he told her.

The wind mage did not carry his voice into the lamps this time. The words belonged to the junction and the people forced to witness it.

Sela looked up at him. “He healed my boy.”

“An unknown caster altered your child with an unlicensed discipline. You have no means of knowing what was done.”

“I know he can use his hand.”

A murmur moved through the crowd.

Solmir let it. Perhaps he thought the sentence beneath it favored him: a poor woman impressed by a dangerous trick, unable to understand the risk.

“Tell me where the healing occurred,” he said. “Name the practitioner who brought you there, and the judgment can be reviewed.”

Nema.

Orin’s fingers dug into the roof tile.

Sela looked at the black bands around her wrists.

Then she looked at the people gathered beyond the earth barriers.

“No.”

Solmir returned to his staff.

Orin did not know whether Sela had protected Nema because she loved her, owed her, feared the Wardens, or simply understood what a name would cost.

It did not matter.

She had refused while kneeling inside the consequence.

The district bell began to move.

Orin heard its mechanism before the sound: a massive iron complaint from the western tower, followed by the rush of air displaced by the descending weight.

Solmir lifted his staff.

The black-glass rings along it separated.

Fire entered the lowest ring. Wind filled the second. A white-blue lightning path leaped between the third and the lamps around the junction.

The restraints answered.

The first lightning fork brightened.

The second followed.

The third did not wait for it.

“Changed,” Orin said. “They’re running together.”

Ultimos closed his hand around the copper line.

White light appeared beneath his smallest finger.

It entered the metal without spreading across its surface. Orin saw it only where the dark copper had cracked, a thread passing through the message channel toward the junction.

The fourth fork ignited.

Black fire filled the band around Sela’s waist.

She arched against the restraints.

Pale tracing exposed the incomplete path’s return beneath the paving.

It did not travel through the silver lines on the street. It descended through the platform, crossed the buried chain pit, and rose inside the monument. The carved black archmage breaking the white hand was the final anchor of the execution spell.

The Conclave had made its old lie part of the killing circuit.

Ultimos saw it through the copper at the same moment Orin did.

The light beneath his finger divided.

One line followed the signal toward Sela. Another descended through the roof, crossed the cooperage wall, and entered the buried network beneath the street. They did not attack the black spell. They ran beside it, mapping every protected join as the lethal instruction completed itself.

Solmir’s staff struck the stone.

“Carry out the sentence.”

At the eastern post, an earth mage looked at Sela instead of the Archmage. The brown light around his hands weakened for half a breath.

Then training—or fear—restored it.

The district bell sounded.

Sela screamed.

Ultimos drew one finger across the copper.

A ring of white geometry closed beneath the platform.

It was no shield around flesh. The fire instruction reached the point where it should have entered Sela’s restraints and found a second path already waiting. Meridian light held that path in a rigid loop, complete enough for the black working to accept and empty enough to contain nothing it could kill.

Fire rushed through the loop.

The bands around Sela blazed red.

Her scream stopped.

The execution spell continued reporting success.

Ultimos’s other hand opened.

White lines found the earth anchors inside the two posts. Each anchor carried Solmir’s active claim and the resistance of every Warden feeding the junction. The first line struck and bent. The second split into three thinner edges. Tile cracked beneath Ultimos’s boots.

He could not simply move a protected working. He had to remove the protections that made it belong to someone else.

Below, Solmir turned toward the cooperage.

“Interference,” he said.

The word entered the Warden formation like a blade.

Every head lifted.

The two Wardens on the neighboring roof saw the white thread inside the false gutter.

“There!”

Wind crossed the gap.

Orin threw himself behind the chimney. A transparent edge shaved through the upper bricks and filled the air with red fragments. One struck his shoulder hard enough to spin him. Another opened his cheek.

Ultimos did not raise a barrier.

He could not.

Both hands were occupied. The closed loop beneath Sela required one continuous structure. The other hand was separating the earth anchors while Solmir and the formation tried to hold them.

For the first time since the ruins, Orin watched an attack approach Ultimos without being dismissed.

The older Warden drew both arms back for a second strike.

“Ultimos!”

The first earth anchor broke.

Not the post. Not the platform. Only the magical relationship fixing the restraint to both.

The second followed.

The bands around Sela became one unprotected structure carrying active magic.

Ultimos seized it.

Every restraint moved at once.

They did not tear Sela upward or drag her across the boards. The metal bands lifted her weight by fractions while a plane of white light opened beneath her knees. The four wooden pins Kisaya had loosened slid from their brackets. The boards separated along their fitted edges and folded down into the abandoned chain pit.

Sela vanished through the floor.

The second wind edge reached the cooperage.

Ultimos released the copper line and turned.

One dismissive burst of pale light opened the spell from end to end. Wind struck the roof as harmless pressure. Chimney dust rolled past Orin’s face.

Before the Wardens could cast again, a plane of white light unfolded between the cooperage chimney and the neighboring ridge. Its edges locked into brick and tile. The next current flattened against it and dispersed above the alley.

Ultimos closed one finger. The anchored plane remained.

The white loop beneath the platform collapsed.

Black fire poured into nothing.

The lamps around the junction went dark.

In the chain pit, Kisaya caught Sela badly.

Orin could not see the impact, but he heard both of them strike the old timber braces below. Kisaya swore. Sela made a broken sound that proved she was still conscious.

Ultimos lowered two fingers.

The restraints released their final red light and came apart into separate bands before touching the ground. None vanished. None needed to. Their magical relationships had been dismantled, leaving ordinary black metal to fall around Sela’s feet.

“Move!” Kisaya shouted from beneath the platform.

Sela answered with something Orin could not hear.

Then two sets of footsteps fled into the warming conduit.

The black spell continued to burn uselessly through the monument for three more heartbeats.

Solmir struck his staff against the road and severed it himself.

Silence reached the junction in pieces.

First the bell ended.

Then the empty restraint bands stopped falling.

Then the crowd understood that the woman who had occupied the platform was gone.

Solmir looked into the open chain pit.

“Seal the eastern conduit.”

Wardens moved.

Earth rose across the bakery lane. Wind mages launched from the roofs. The formation had spent its first motion turning toward the place where Sela disappeared, exactly as Ultimos had intended.

He stepped away from the cooperage gutter.

“Stay here,” he told Orin.

“Where are you going?”

Ultimos crossed the ridge without answering.

Orin crawled to the broken side of the chimney. Blood from his cheek reached his mouth. Below, a Warden dropped into the chain pit and found only the dismantled restraints and the dark opening of the conduit.

Kisaya had already taken Sela beyond the first turn.

The east side of the junction filled with black fire and shouted orders.

On the west, the surrender circle waited empty.

A man in a damaged charcoal coat entered from the north.

He came through the crowd rather than the prepared circle. People moved aside before they knew why. The repaired coat opened as he walked, no longer hiding the breadth of the white armor beneath it. Evening light caught the gauntlets. The deep hood fell back.

The smooth white helmet and its red cross faced the Archmage of Veyr.

Ultimos stopped beyond the outer edge of the containment geometry.

Sela was gone. The eastern formation had broken. The surrender circle held enough force to crush a tower and nothing inside it.

Only then did he answer the summons.

“You called,” Ultimos said. “Speak.”

The words did not travel through the lamps.

They did not need to.

The crowd had become quiet enough to hear the faint scrape of metal as Solmir adjusted his grip on the staff.

Wardens turned from the eastern conduit. Those nearest Ultimos raised their hands, but no one attacked. Their formation had been built around the black circle, and he stood beyond it with a stone barrier between himself and the closest anchored mage.

Solmir recovered first.

“You entered a civic memorial, assaulted its security, killed officers of Veyr, sabotaged the containment network, and interfered with a lawful sentence.”

His voice carried through the opened focus beside the platform. Every lamp in the district repeated the charges.

Ultimos glanced once at the broken boards where Sela had been.

“Your lawful sentence required the death of a woman whose child I healed.”

“It required the cooperation of a material witness. You made her relevant when you performed prohibited magic upon her son.”

Orin felt the answer strike the roof beneath him.

Solmir had found the same chain of responsibility he and Kisaya had used against Ultimos. He simply believed it ended somewhere else.

“You fastened her into a killing spell,” Ultimos said.

“I offered you the opportunity to prevent it.”

“You offered obedience.”

No anger entered Ultimos’s voice. The absence of it made the street feel colder.

“You received an answer.”

The black bands scattered beneath the platform testified more clearly than anything else could.

Solmir’s gaze traveled over the white armor. Public notices had shown only a frightened witness’s sketch. The Hall reports might describe the red cross, the gauntlets, the impossible absence left by the security captain.

The mural behind those reports showed the same helmet.

Orin watched the Archmage construct his disbelief in front of the city.

“A costume copied from the Hall,” Solmir said. “An artifact providing effects its bearer does not understand. Perhaps an armored construct released when the ruins were breached.”

A ripple passed through the crowd. Those explanations were frightening. They were also possible in ways that a man surviving four centuries was not.

“You named me white mage before you named yourself,” Ultimos said.

“I used the superstition spreading through my streets to address the criminal exploiting it.”

“Then you do not believe I am what you called.”

Solmir lowered the base of his staff to the paving. “I believe you are responsible for a catastrophic magical event at a prohibited site, the deaths of six civic officers, an attack upon the Hall of Liberation, and the abduction of two witnesses.”

Six.

The three beneath the ruins. The captain and lightning mage in the Hall. The witch in the maintenance corridor. Solmir had gathered the separate scenes into one charge before Orin had learned how to sleep after any of them.

Numbers had already begun turning the last two days into an official shape.

“They came with you willingly,” Ultimos said.

“Children pursued by the law are not competent to choose the armed adult controlling them.”

Orin almost laughed.

It would have sounded too much like panic, so he kept his mouth closed.

Kisaya would have enjoyed explaining how little control anyone possessed over her. She was beneath the street with Sela, however, moving toward a route that might already be closing.

Orin looked toward the bakery lane.

The earth barrier there rose another foot.

Solmir noticed the movement of his head.

His eyes settled on the cooperage roof.

“Orin,” he said.

Hearing his name in the Archmage’s voice was worse than hearing it read from a wanted notice.

“The woman is no longer useful to him. You still are. Come down from the roof, and the charge of assisting a severe magical threat will be reviewed.”

Ultimos did not look back at Orin.

He did not need to order him to remain.

Sela had been offered the same review.

Orin stayed behind the broken chimney.

“He has answered you,” Ultimos said.

“He has said nothing.”

“Precisely.”

The first genuine fracture appeared in Solmir’s composure. It lasted only an instant, a tightening beside his mouth, but the Wardens nearest him saw it and adjusted their stances.

Black fire gathered inside the lowest ring of his staff.

“Remove the helmet.”

The order crossed the junction.

Ultimos did not move.

“If you are human,” Solmir continued, “show the people whose safety you claim to value the face of the man asking them to accept this violence.”

Ultimos’s attention settled upon the black-glass rings enclosing Solmir’s staff.

“You require my face to sustain your explanation?”

“I require your compliance.”

Orin stared down at the red cross.

He had traveled from the ruins to the center of Veyr and beneath half the city beside Ultimos. He had watched him restore flesh, erase bodies, mourn a painted woman, and describe revenge large enough to frighten the dark.

He had never seen his face.

Until that moment, the absence had felt like part of the armor. Ultimos possessed a voice, hands, choices, and a terrible history. A face seemed almost unnecessary.

Now every person in the junction waited for it.

Ultimos raised both hands.

His fingers entered the fur-lined mantle and found two catches beneath the lower edge of the helmet.

Orin stopped breathing.

One catch released.

The sound was quiet. Solmir heard it anyway.

The uppermost ring of black glass around his staff rotated toward Ultimos.

Its curved surface flattened.

A silver iris opened at its center.

The lamps around the junction dimmed as the staff drew their sight inward. Whatever the glass recorded would not remain in the Ashward. It would be carried through the network, toward the Spire and whatever waited inside its restricted rooms.

Ultimos released the second catch.

The helmet shifted by less than the width of a finger.

Darkness appeared between white metal and the high collar of his armor.

Solmir leaned forward.

It was not the movement of a man waiting to discover whether a construct was empty.

It was anticipation.

Ultimos became motionless with both hands against the helmet.

The silver iris focused.

Pale light appeared along the released seam—and vanished.

Ultimos pressed the helmet back into place.

The first catch closed.

Then the second.

A breath passed through the crowd, hundreds of people releasing the same denied expectation.

Solmir’s staff remained aimed at the red cross.

“Remove it,” he said again.

Ultimos lowered his hands.

“That glass was dormant when I entered the street.”

Solmir’s expression did not change. “It is an evidence focus.”

“It did not open for my armor. It did not open for my magic. It opened when I touched the helmet.”

“Because I ordered your identification recorded.”

“No.” Ultimos studied the silver iris. “A recording focus would have observed me from the moment I arrived. Yours is prepared to isolate a face.”

The Archmage’s fingers tightened around the staff.

Ultimos stepped forward.

The Wardens nearest him flared their wards. He stopped before any of them could decide that one pace constituted an attack.

“You called me an impostor,” he said. “Yet you prepared an instrument to compare what lies beneath this helmet with something already in your possession.”

“Veyr possesses records of many historical criminals.”

The answer came too quickly.

People in the crowd looked toward the monument. Its carved white tyrant possessed no face at all, only the monstrous hand the archmage had broken.

Ultimos looked at it too.

“The public mural shows my helmet.”

Solmir said nothing.

“Your schoolbooks show the cross. Your monuments show an emblem. None of them show the man beneath it.”

The silver iris in the staff closed.

Too late.

Solmir had begun the encounter by forcing Ultimos into the light. Now every silence pulled something hidden out of the Archmage instead.

Ultimos’s voice remained quiet.

“Whose face,” he asked, “were you waiting to see?”

End of Chapter Seven
