Volume One · Chapter Eight

Form Against Element

Ultimos

Solmir answered with the smallest movement of his hand.

The silver iris folded shut. Black glass curved over it until the upper ring of his staff looked seamless again.

For three heartbeats, nobody in the old gatehouse junction spoke.

Then the Archmage turned his wrist.

“Take him alive,” he said. “Do not damage the helmet.”

The Wardens moved before the last word reached the roofs.

Earth struck first.

The paving rose around Ultimos’s boots in four fitted blocks. Frost spread from channels hidden beneath the street, climbing his greaves and closing over the joints at his knees. Wind tightened around his arms with enough force to drag the damaged charcoal coat flat against his armor. White-blue current leaped from the nearest restraint post and entered the ice.

The formation had been waiting beneath the junction from the beginning. Solmir had only changed its center.

Orin gripped the broken cooperage chimney and watched lightning cover Ultimos from heel to shoulder.

The crowd cried out. Several people tried to run, but the earth barriers at the four streets left them only the routes the Wardens had chosen. Black-coated officers shouted for everyone to move north and west. Their commands collided with one another beneath the fading district bell.

Ultimos remained where he stood.

Lightning crawled over the smooth white helmet. The red cross disappeared beneath its glare.

Solmir raised his staff another inch. “Release the armor and place your hands where they can be seen.”

Pale lines appeared at Ultimos’s feet.

They did not spread into a circle or oppose the force around him. One entered the seam between his left greave and the stone holding it. Another climbed the edge where ice had fixed itself across his knee. Two more extended into the air beside his shoulders, so fine that Orin saw them only when the lightning made them cast shadows.

Ultimos opened his hands.

The paving lost its grip.

Four blocks broke into ordinary pieces around his boots. The ice separated from the armor along a perfect boundary and fell in heavy plates, still frozen. Wind rushed past him in every direction at once. Lightning followed the pale line descending from his heel and vanished into the buried chain pit with a crack that shook soot from the monument.

The red cross faced Solmir again.

“You prepared an answer for my face,” Ultimos said. “You may provide it before I remove yours.”

One of the Wardens near the platform flinched.

Solmir did not.

He lowered his staff to the paving.

The broken stone around Ultimos’s feet shone brown.

Ice surged from the northern lamp instead of the channels below. Wind returned from behind him. Lightning crossed the silver lines laid along the street, divided at three restraint posts, and reached him through the scattered pieces of earth.

The formation rebuilt itself without asking the same anchors twice.

Ultimos severed the wind at his right shoulder. Pressure returned against his left. He opened the lightning path beneath the street, and the nearest Warden’s chain flashed as another path accepted the burden. The ice on one gauntlet cracked; frost closed over the other.

For the first time, he did not end a spell as quickly as it reached him.

Orin could not see his expression. Solmir could not either.

That did not prevent the Archmage from smiling.

“The Hall report described what you do,” Solmir said. His voice passed through the proclamation focus and entered the lamps again. “You break a working at its visible point and allow superstition to explain the rest.”

Ultimos placed two fingers against the band of ice around his wrist.

His attention traveled beyond it.

Orin had learned to recognize the posture. Ultimos was no longer looking at the thing he touched. He was following every relationship that told it how to remain.

The ice split and dropped from his arm.

It began forming again from the roof braces above the western street.

“Better,” Ultimos said.

The single word removed Solmir’s smile.

Black fire brightened along the Archmage’s staff. “Second formation. Close every transfer.”

Wardens changed positions throughout the junction. Some touched the open lamps. Others fastened their chains to iron rings set into walls and paving. On the rooftops, wind mages drove narrow black spikes between tiles. Colored light moved through the district in branching pulses, and each pulse ended somewhere different from the last.

It was the most complicated spell Orin had ever seen.

It was also doing something to the plane in front of him.

The white barrier still stretched from the cooperage chimney to the neighboring ridge. Ultimos had locked its edges into brick and tile before descending. Wind flattened against it without passing through, but the roof beneath its eastern corner gave a low, splintering complaint.

Two Wardens on the next building were not attacking the plane.

They were attacking the place holding it.

Air slid under the tiles. Nails lifted. Old mortar spilled from the ridge in a red stream.

The eastern edge of the plane trembled.

Orin looked down at Ultimos.

Ice and lightning continued closing around the white armor. Ultimos’s left hand separated one lattice while his right traced the path by which it returned. He had not looked toward the roof.

“The roof!”

The wind Wardens tore the ridge away.

The anchored edge of the white plane remained perfectly straight for half a breath with nothing beneath it.

Then the geometry broke.

Light folded toward the missing corner and disappeared. Wind crossed the roof in its place.

Orin threw himself behind the chimney. Air struck the bricks, entered the crack made by the earlier blade, and pulled the upper half apart.

The chimney came down around him.

He covered his head. A brick hit his forearm. Another burst against the roof beside his ear. Dust swallowed the junction, the Wardens, and the red cross below.

Orin crawled before he could see where he was going.

The cooperage roof sloped toward a loading court on its southern side. Kisaya had shown him the hatch years ago, after Ressa’s crew discovered the owner had nailed it shut from beneath and declared that someone smaller ought to fix the problem. The replacement boards still differed in color from the rest.

He found them with both hands.

A Warden landed on the ridge behind him.

“Orin! Stay where you are.”

Orin drove his shoulder into the boards.

They did not move.

Of course they did not. Being shown a useful exit several years earlier was not the same as checking whether somebody had nailed it shut again.

The Warden crossed the broken tiles. Wind gathered around her hand, contained in a narrow band rather than shaped into a cutting edge.

They still wanted him alive.

That knowledge should have been comforting.

It was not.

Orin hooked his fingers beneath one warped plank and pulled until a nail tore through the wood. The opening beneath smelled of sawdust, old glue, and the bitter oil used to seal barrel hoops.

The Warden caught the back of his coat.

White light passed through the roof between them.

It was no wider than a thread. It touched the metal clasp at the Warden’s wrist, opened the relationship holding her wind in a closed band, and continued into the tiles without harming either of them.

The released air threw her backward.

Orin went through the hatch headfirst.

He struck a rack, bounced off two empty casks, and landed on the cooperage floor with enough noise to offend every craftsperson who had ever arranged a workspace.

Above him, the Warden shouted for the lower court to be sealed.

Orin pushed himself upright.

The cooperage doors stood open onto the junction. Workers had abandoned the building so quickly that a shaving curl still hung from a drawknife clamped to the central bench. Beyond the doors, people pressed behind an earth barrier while Wardens tried to move them away from the fighting.

The nearest street lamp flashed amber.

Orin stopped.

The pulse entered the western barrier, crossed beneath the street, and disappeared toward the southern lane. It was the same transfer warning printed beside the evacuation diagrams at ward school: one branch releasing its burden before another accepted it.

The earth wall in front of the crowd sank six inches.

People surged toward the opening.

The lamp flashed again.

“Wait!” Orin shouted.

No one heard him over the fighting.

He ran through the cooperage doors.

The older wind Warden from the roof had reached the other side of the barrier. She was using both hands to direct the crowd. When she saw Orin emerge, one hand moved toward the metal clasp Ultimos had opened at her wrist.

Then a man behind her lost his footing and struck the wall, and she looked back at the people instead.

Her attention sharpened.

“The south wall is taking the transfer,” Orin said. “If they enter that lane, it’ll close on them.”

“Get on the ground.”

The lamp gave a third amber pulse.

Orin pointed. “Look at it!”

The Warden looked.

Across the junction, the southern barrier rose from the street.

Two men already inside the lane stumbled backward. Earth closed between them and the rest of the crowd, leaving them trapped on the wrong side.

The older Warden swore and changed the movement of her hands.

“North passage!” she shouted. Wind carried the order over the crowd. “Keep them against the cooperage wall. Nobody enters a lane until I mark it.”

She turned back toward Orin.

For a moment, law, recognition, and the people crushing against the barrier competed across her face.

The people won.

“If you can read another transfer,” she said, “you tell me before it happens.”

Orin nodded.

“Then stay where I can see you.”

He did not promise.

In the center of the junction, Ultimos pulled his hand free of a ring of ice.

Pale geometry moved from wrist to wrist through the Warden formation. Each time it found an anchor, three more accepted the spell before he could separate it. The work no longer looked effortless. He had to follow the transfers, let them reveal their protections, and choose which relationship to break first.

Solmir stood behind overlapping barriers and watched him learn.

“An artifact repeats what it was made to do,” the Archmage said. “A construct adapts within the limits of its design. You have demonstrated neither a man nor a miracle.”

“You prepared a face,” Ultimos replied.

“I prepared evidence.”

“Then show it to them.”

The nearest lamps carried the question farther than Solmir’s answer.

He lifted his staff.

The formation changed.

Wind struck the roof of a boarded warehouse east of the junction. It did not shape itself into a blade. It entered beneath the slate and accelerated every loose piece toward the crowd.

Then the spell ended.

Hundreds of tiles continued moving.

Ultimos turned from the bindings around his arms.

White planes opened above the street. Slate shattered against them in dark waves. The first plane held. The second bent at its unsupported center. The third formed late, and three tiles passed beneath its edge.

One struck a Warden’s ward and broke. Another buried itself in the side of an empty cart.

The last spun toward a woman carrying a child.

Ultimos extended two fingers.

A narrow frame of light closed around the tile before it reached her. Its rotation stopped inside four rigid edges. The frame dropped to the paving and released an ordinary piece of slate.

The ice around Ultimos’s left leg thickened while he was turned away.

Solmir saw it.

Fire crossed the northern roof.

The spell burned only long enough to ignite the dry pitch beneath its shingles. By the time Ultimos opened the lattice that had carried it, ordinary flames had already taken hold.

“Buckets!” someone shouted.

A water mage pulled a twisting column from a public main. Before it reached the roof, lightning struck the iron collar around the pipe. Metal split. The water spell collapsed.

The broken main did not stop.

Water erupted from the paving, knocked two Wardens from their feet, and rushed toward the lowered northern barrier where the crowd had been directed.

Orin watched the lamp.

Red entered its lowest channel. Amber crossed above it. The signal divided toward the cooperage and the northern lane.

“The west barrier is releasing!” he shouted.

The older Warden repeated the warning through the wind. Earth workers lowered the western wall at her signal before the transfer could trap the crowd against it.

People moved through the gap as water spread across the junction.

Ultimos raised his hand toward the burning roof.

For an instant, Orin expected the shingles to repair themselves beneath the fire.

They did not.

White lines divided the roof into sections. Burning slate and timber separated from the unburned structure along joints Ultimos could see. A rigid frame caught the loosened section and held it above the building while the bucket line drowned it.

The rest of the roof sagged.

Pale supports unfolded beneath its rafters.

Each answer remained after the immediate danger passed. Planes covered the crowd. Frames held timber and slate. Supports braced the roof. Fine lines still moved through the spell anchors around Ultimos’s body.

The junction filled with white geometry one obligation at a time.

Solmir lowered his staff toward the road.

Earth moved beneath the warehouse.

It pulled one foundation pier sideways and stopped.

The spell had ended. The building continued to fall.

“Clear!” the older Warden shouted.

The warehouse leaned over the northern lane.

Ultimos closed his raised hand.

The frame holding the burned roof vanished. So did two planes above the street. White pillars appeared beneath the warehouse eaves, accepted the descending weight, and drove luminous roots across the paving.

The building struck them hard enough to make every line flare.

It stopped halfway down.

Inside the lane, a Warden lay beneath a broken cart. The woman beside him tried to lift the axle and could not.

The older Warden vaulted the barrier.

“Hold the route,” another officer called.

She ignored him and ran to the cart.

Orin followed.

Together with two laborers, they dragged the axle high enough for the trapped man to pull one leg free. His coat was black and his ward still glowed weakly around his shoulders. Blood covered the paving beneath his knee.

Ultimos looked toward him once.

He did not abandon the supports, the barriers, and the restraints closing around him to cross the street and heal a man whose companions could carry him.

Instead, he turned his helmet toward Solmir.

“Your capture has begun striking your own people.”

“You brought an unclassified catastrophic threat into an occupied district.”

“I entered the street alone.”

Black fire climbed Solmir’s staff. “And arrived with the power to remove a civic officer from the world.”

The injured Warden cried out as his companions lifted him.

Solmir did not look away from the red cross.

Something struck beneath the eastern lane.

The paving rose and settled.

Orin turned toward the open chain pit.

The warming conduit lay below that street.

“Kisaya.”

He left the injured Warden with the laborers and ran around the broken platform. The nearest street had been sealed by a wall of earth twice his height. Black fire shone inside cracks across its surface. There was no opening into the pit now; a stone plug filled the passage where Kisaya and Sela had disappeared.

Orin pressed one hand to it.

He felt another impact through the earth.

Then three quick knocks.

A pause.

One more.

Kisaya was alive.

She was also on the wrong side of several tons of stone.

Orin searched the buildings along the lane. Bakery. Shuttered tool shop. A narrow brick room with a rusted flue rising from its roof.

The warming conduit had once carried heat.

It would need access to furnaces.

He ran to the brick room and found its door chained from outside.

“Kisaya!”

Something slammed into the other side.

The rusted flue coughed soot over his head.

Orin pulled at the chain. The links held. He looked for a latch, a loose hinge, anything Kisaya would have already found if she were standing where he was.

The pale disk was with her.

The thought arrived without permission. White Order service doors had answered it beneath the northern reservoir. If an old furnace access carried the same mark—

The door burst outward.

Orin jumped aside.

Kisaya came through shoulder first with soot across half her face. Her loosened braid had almost ceased to be a braid. One sleeve remained bound against her injured arm, and the other supported Sela around the waist.

Sela’s right foot dragged. Dust covered her hair and the bruise at her mouth had darkened, but she was upright.

Behind them, an iron service plate hung open inside the furnace room. Its lock had been removed with a pry bar, three pieces of brick, and enough force to leave Kisaya’s knuckles bleeding.

Her jacket remained fastened over the place where she carried the disk.

“You took your time,” Kisaya said.

Orin stared at the broken lock. “I was outside.”

“Comfortable, was it?”

A lightning strike turned the junction white.

Neither of them continued.

Sela gripped Orin’s shoulder. “My boy.”

“He wasn’t here,” Orin said. “They didn’t bring him.”

Relief almost folded her. Kisaya tightened her hold.

The nearby lamp flashed amber.

Orin looked past them toward the earth barriers. “The streets are transferring in order. I can tell you which one opens, but only for a few breaths.”

Kisaya examined the crowd, the damaged warehouse, and the chain pit beneath the ruined platform.

“The old pits connect under the monument,” she said. “If the north access isn’t filled, we can move people beneath the junction and out through the tannery court.”

“Can Sela make it?”

Sela answered before Kisaya could. “I can make it away from him.”

She was looking at Solmir.

The Archmage stood inside three overlapping wards while his officers and the unknown white mage tore the junction apart around him.

Kisaya followed her gaze.

Some of the satisfaction that had once entered her face whenever Ultimos humiliated a Warden was still there.

It no longer stood alone.

“Get her to the pit,” Orin said. “I’ll watch the lamps.”

Kisaya looked toward Ultimos.

Ice had sealed one of his arms to his side. Two white supports remained beneath the warehouse. A third held the cooperage roof where Orin’s barrier had torn it open. Light covered half the junction, and every piece of it seemed to ask for a different part of his attention.

Solmir saw the same thing.

The Archmage lifted the black-glass staff above his head.

“Open the northern weather cell.”

Several Wardens hesitated.

The older wind Warden who had helped the injured officer turned from the crowd. “Archmage, the district is not cleared.”

“Then clear it.”

“The eastern passages are sealed.”

Solmir’s gaze moved to Ultimos. “Open the cell.”

Black-glass rings throughout the junction rotated at once.

The elemental colors inside the lamps went dark. Their black seals remained open.

Wind descended from a sky that had been almost still.

It entered the old gatehouse through every street, pulling smoke, dust, water spray, and loose scraps of cloth toward the center. Rooftop conductors bent inward. Blue current moved along them without thunder.

Something pressed against the air above the junction.

Orin had seen drawings of storm spirits in academy castoffs. The illustrations gave them mouths, claws, and convenient bodies that could be labeled. The thing descending over the gatehouse possessed none of those kindnesses.

Cloud folded into itself around an absence. Lightning revealed long curves that vanished whenever Orin tried to follow them. Wind circled a center that moved with intention rather than weather. Each time the black-glass rings pulsed, the presence above them tightened as if an invisible chain had been drawn through its spine.

A sound entered the junction.

It resembled thunder heard from beneath deep water.

People screamed and pushed toward the barriers.

Kisaya dragged open the nearest chain-pit grate. “Orin!”

He watched the lamp housing beside it. No light remained inside the glass, but the silver transfer lines below still carried a pulse.

“Not yet.”

The storm spirit descended.

Solmir pointed his staff at Ultimos.

The black rings contracted.

Lightning fell with them.

Ultimos tore his arm free of the ice and raised one hand.

A straight line of Meridian light assembled before his palm.

Orin knew what came after that line.

He had watched it erase a witch, cross a buried chamber, and leave nothing behind that could fall.

The storm spirit struck.

The white line did not become a beam.

Ultimos opened his hand.

Concentric rings formed above the junction, one inside another, creating a luminous passage through the descending wind. Lightning struck the first and divided. It struck the second and curved upward. The spirit’s shapeless body entered the passage and convulsed against its black bindings.

Ultimos had found something inside the storm that he would not destroy.

“It is alive,” Orin said.

Kisaya looked up once. “Then help me keep everyone else that way.”

The silver line beneath the pit grate brightened.

“Now.”

Kisaya lowered Sela into the opening. The older Warden reached them before Orin could decide whether to run. Her ward flared around one arm.

Kisaya placed herself between the officer and Sela.

“If you stop us—”

“I’m not stopping you.” The Warden turned toward the crowd. “How many can that passage hold?”

Kisaya’s anger lost its prepared direction.

She looked into the pit. “Not all at once. Keep them along the east wall until I knock twice. No carts. The lower braces are rotten.”

The Warden accepted instructions from a wanted girl without wasting time on the fact.

She began moving people.

Above them, the storm screamed again.

Ultimos’s rings held its outer force while pale lines moved toward the black collar inside it. The binding did not belong to one focus. Roof conductors, street lamps, and the staff in Solmir’s hands each maintained a portion. When Ultimos separated one, the others tightened.

He studied the transfer.

“Eastern weather confinement,” he said. His voice carried through the roar without assistance. “Fourth civic pattern.”

Solmir’s staff jerked toward him.

Ultimos touched two fingers together. A black line inside the spirit opened, then closed through a neighboring anchor.

“Your third relief gate is absent.”

“There is no public designation for that working,” Solmir said.

“No. You removed it with the safeguard.”

The spirit twisted against the luminous passage. Lightning leaped across its inner boundary and split a roof conductor.

Solmir’s face had lost its earlier satisfaction.

He pointed the staff more firmly, as though certainty could be restored by posture.

“You were assembled from material stolen from the restricted archive.”

Ultimos turned the red cross toward him.

“Then you admit the archive remembers me.”

The lamps repeated the words before Solmir could close the proclamation focus.

They traveled into the Ashward.

Perhaps farther.

Orin saw the understanding move through the crowd in fragments. Nobody suddenly accepted that a four-hundred-year-old man stood in the junction. They looked instead at Solmir, because the Archmage had just admitted there were records capable of making such a man.

Solmir struck the base of his staff against the paving.

Every black collar within the storm tightened.

Ultimos’s rings flared.

The supports beneath the leaning warehouse dimmed.

“He’s making him choose,” Orin said.

Kisaya looked from the storm to the building.

Another foundation pier shifted beneath the warehouse. The spell ended immediately. Stone and timber continued downward under their own weight.

At the same time, wind tore the old iron gate from the surviving arch.

The gate accelerated across the junction.

The wind released it before it reached Ultimos.

He could preserve the storm’s living structure.

He could hold the warehouse above the evacuation route.

He could turn and impose a new path on several hundred pounds of ordinary iron already moving faster than a cart.

For one terrible instant, Orin understood the order in which Ultimos chose.

The pale supports beneath the warehouse brightened.

One black collar opened inside the storm.

The iron gate struck Ultimos across the left shoulder.

White armor and iron hit the remains of the ancient arch together.

Stone exploded outward.

The storm passage wavered. One ring failed. Lightning reached the paving and threw Wardens and civilians from their feet.

Orin lost sight of Ultimos behind dust and fallen masonry.

“Move!” the older Warden shouted.

Kisaya dropped into the chain pit beside Sela. Orin helped the first child down after them, then an old man whose hands shook too badly to find the ladder.

The warehouse supports held.

The storm remained inside its narrowing passage.

Ultimos stepped out of the dust.

The charcoal coat had torn away from his left side. A crack crossed the broad white pauldron from collar to outer edge, exposing a dark underlayer of armor beneath it. The helmet remained sealed. Its smooth face had acquired a gray streak where stone struck it, but the red cross was unbroken.

He lifted his damaged shoulder once.

The pauldron drew together by a fraction, enough to keep the crack from spreading. He did not spend another moment repairing it.

Solmir stared.

Ultimos raised both hands.

The luminous passage contracted around the storm. Not upon the living center—around the black relationships forcing it downward. Pale edges entered each collar at once. They separated command from containment, pressure from direction, punishment from restraint.

The spirit surged upward.

For a breath, it could have fallen upon the Wardens who had bound it.

It did not obey Ultimos any more than it obeyed Solmir.

The white rings opened toward the sky. The storm followed the only unobstructed path, tore three roof conductors from their mounts, and vanished into the evening clouds with a final report that rattled every window in the Ashward.

Rain began falling over the junction.

Only there.

Large, cold drops struck hot roof tiles and turned dust to dark streaks on Ultimos’s armor.

Solmir looked up at the empty sky.

His staff still contained fire, earth, ice, wind, and lightning. Wardens still held every street. The district network still answered him.

Orin saw when the Archmage understood that none of those facts had produced control.

Fear reached him without making him retreat.

It made him commit everything.

“Primary anchors,” Solmir said. “Join the containment array.”

The older Warden looked over from the evacuation line. “The civic load is already unstable.”

“Join it.”

“Archmage—”

“That is an order.”

She did not return to formation.

Other Wardens did.

Black chains fastened to street rings. Roof mages drove their conductors through tile and beam into the walls beneath. Water shone inside the ruptured main and spread into silver channels across the paving. Fire entered the proclamation platform. Ice gathered along the earth barriers, hardening them into a single perimeter.

Lightning connected every part.

The arrangement surrounding Ultimos changed from a collection of attacks into one enormous working.

Orin recognized the foundations and nothing more. Earth carried the weight. Wind defined pressure. Lightning allowed distant pieces to answer together. Fire and ice occupied alternating bands around the junction, each held behind the other until the array closed.

He could follow the signals.

He could not imagine what they would do when released.

Ultimos could.

He watched the outer ring cross the old gatehouse foundations. His helmet turned toward the lamps, the water lines, the roof conductors, and the black channels disappearing beneath the surrounding streets.

“This did not exist in my age,” he said.

Solmir drew himself upright.

“The world did not stop when yours did.”

Ultimos gave no answer.

The rain ran from the lower edge of his helmet.

He extended one hand toward the nearest earth barrier without touching it. The black light beneath the stone continued beyond the junction. It entered homes, workshops, public mains, foundation braces, and every lamp still carrying the district order.

“You have made the Ashward your final anchor.”

“The Ashward is what this array protects.”

“If one burden transfers beyond its capacity, the district receives it.”

“It will hold.”

Ultimos lowered his hand. “You do not know that.”

The words carried no accusation.

That made them sound like a sentence pronounced by someone qualified to judge the work.

Solmir’s jaw tightened.

He brought the staff down.

The array closed.

Wind compressed the center of the junction. Earth rose beneath Ultimos in concentric rings. Fire entered the space between them and ice sealed its outer boundary, trapping heat and pressure inside a shrinking shell. Lightning struck every layer too quickly for any single connection to be separated before the next accepted it.

The street disappeared inside black elemental light.

The force lifted pieces of broken paving and held them against the shell. The monument groaned. Water retreated through the ruptured main as the array pulled upon every connected structure for stability.

Orin felt the chain-pit ladder jump beneath his hand.

The old man below him cried out. Kisaya shouted for everyone to get clear of the braces.

Across the junction, one roof conductor tore loose.

Its burden entered the nearest lamp.

The lamp burst.

Black fire raced along the cooperage wall.

The older Warden flattened a wind ward between the flames and the remaining crowd. Fire streamed upward along the compressed air. The ward shuddered immediately. She held it anyway.

Inside the array, white light appeared.

It began at Ultimos’s feet.

Lines crossed the paving in precise angles, passing through the empty surrender circle and the scattered restraint bands. They climbed the surviving gatehouse stones. They entered the old chain pits, the open lamps, and the silver channels laid for Sela’s execution. Vertical planes rose from them, thin enough that the elemental colors remained visible through their surfaces.

More geometry joined overhead.

The old gatehouse junction stood inside a luminous description of itself.

Orin saw the road divided into fitted layers. The proclamation platform became intersecting edges and load-bearing points. White lines traced the warehouse supports, the cooperage wall, the ruptured water main, and every active path by which Solmir’s array transferred force.

The construction did not continue through the whole Ashward.

It ended at the four streets.

Ultimos had drawn a boundary around what he had spent the battle learning.

He stopped speaking.

One movement of his left hand closed pale frames beneath the roofs already failing.

His right separated the nearest distributed anchors from one another. Black force attempted to transfer and found white divisions already placed between every route.

Then the empty surrender circle changed.

The trap Solmir had prepared to crush anything inside it opened downward. Ultimos joined the array’s active paths to that waiting structure and gave their force somewhere to complete itself without entering the district.

Fire plunged into the circle.

Wind followed.

Ice shattered inward and vanished beneath the street in a roar of steam.

Lightning filled every pale plane at once.

The geometry shook.

Ultimos’s cracked pauldron opened again.

The white reinforcement along his armor dimmed until Orin could see scorched metal beneath it. Every defense around his body had become less important than the construction surrounding the junction.

Solmir saw the opening.

He pulled the upper rings of his staff apart and drove a spear of black fire through the collapsing array.

It crossed the white description before Ultimos could turn.

The strike hit his breastplate.

Fire wrapped the white armor and drove him back one step.

The construction above the warehouse bent.

Orin held the edge of the chain pit so tightly the broken stone entered his palm.

Ultimos did not look at Solmir.

He closed both hands.

Every white division in the junction became complete.

The distributed anchors lost the ability to pass their burdens. Roof conductors went dark. Warden chains fell from the street rings. Fire separated from the paths feeding it and remained only where ordinary material had already begun to burn. Lightning found the empty surrender circuit and exhausted itself beneath the road.

The master array ended from its edges toward the center.

Wind stopped.

Ice struck the paving in broken sheets.

Earth barriers collapsed into piles of stone.

Water poured from the damaged main and continued pouring, because no magic remained inside it and nothing had repaired the pipe.

For a moment, the loudest sound in the junction was rain striking fire.

Ultimos released the three-dimensional construction.

Most of the light disappeared.

The frames beneath the damaged roofs remained. So did the supports holding the warehouse above the evacuation route. They were closed, anchored structures now, reduced to the few obligations that still required them.

Wardens stared at dead focuses in their hands.

The older woman lowered her failing wind ward and immediately began directing people toward the chain pit again.

Others joined her. A water mage knelt beside the ruptured main and tried to close it with ordinary clamps. Two fire workers climbed toward the northern roof carrying hooks and wet blankets because their spells no longer answered.

The city network had gone dead inside the junction.

Its people continued working.

At the western edge, Solmir lifted his staff.

Nothing entered it.

The black glass reflected Ultimos walking toward him.

Solmir stepped back inside his wards. Their surfaces remained visible, but the network supporting them had gone dark. He forced fire into the lowest ring from his own binding and raised the staff with both hands.

Ultimos extended one finger.

Four planes of light closed around the Archmage.

They did not touch him. They formed a clear enclosure wide enough for him to move, its lower edges anchored into the dismantled surrender circle.

Solmir sent fire through the nearest wall.

The spell entered the pale surface, exposed every active relationship maintaining it, and came apart into heat without crossing.

He tried lightning.

It lasted less time.

The enclosure did not take his magic from him. It allowed every working to exist until he attempted to extend it beyond the boundary, then dismantled the structure at the crossing point.

Solmir struck the wall with his staff.

The black glass rang against light.

Ultimos stopped outside the enclosure.

“You are alive because I require the location of the records you attempted to use against me.”

“You believe this district is the city?” Solmir asked.

Voices rose beyond the four streets.

Fresh Warden formations appeared behind the fallen barriers. Their coats bore silver cords Orin had seen only on officers assigned to the Spire approaches. Earth workers advanced first with portable anchors. Wind mages filled the roofs. Black fire opened above them in broad command seals.

The response had no ancient name.

The lamps that remained intact sounded the ordinary maximum-threat cadence: three low pulses, a pause, then one high.

They were coming because the Archmage had been contained in public and the district network had failed.

That was enough.

Orin climbed out of the chain pit.

Kisaya emerged behind him after settling Sela among the evacuees below. Her face and clothes were black with soot. She looked at the new formations, then at Ultimos.

“That’s more than the Hall sent,” she said.

Orin watched black anchors enter the four streets. “The Spire knows he has Solmir.”

“Can he do that again?”

The remaining white supports trembled above the damaged warehouse.

Ultimos stood straight. Only the broken pauldron and the burn across his breastplate admitted that anything had reached him.

Orin did not know the answer.

Inside the enclosure, Solmir looked toward his approaching officers.

Ultimos reached for the Archmage’s staff.

Solmir tightened both hands around it. Fire surged along the lowest ring and died when its structure touched the enclosure.

The failed spell carried Solmir’s continuing claim into the wall. Pale light followed it back along the staff and opened the ward joining the weapon to its caster.

Ultimos closed his hand.

The weapon tore free of Solmir’s grip and struck the pale wall from inside. One black-glass ring separated from the wood, passed through an opening that existed for less than a heartbeat, and entered Ultimos’s waiting palm.

The silver iris remained shut.

He touched its outer edge with his thumb.

Pale lines traveled over the black glass. A web of silver filaments appeared beneath its surface, open at one end and empty.

“The receiving lattice contains nothing,” Ultimos said.

His thumb moved toward the ring’s center. The pale lines stopped at a deeper chamber that remained dark and sealed.

“The comparison lattice was filled before you entered this district.”

He did not open it.

The featureless helmet turned toward Solmir.

For the first time, the Archmage looked at the red cross without constructing an explanation around it.

The fresh formations reached the edge of the junction. Portable anchors struck the streets one after another.

Solmir’s face had gone pale.

“You cannot be him,” he said.

Ultimos held up the black-glass ring.

“Then why did you bring his face?”

End of Chapter Eight
